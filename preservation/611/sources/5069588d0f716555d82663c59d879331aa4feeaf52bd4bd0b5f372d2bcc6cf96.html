History[edit]

Radio Free Decipher[edit]

(YouTube playlist here.)

Radio Free Decipher (often abbreviated to RFD) was a podcast ran by Decipher, before the term "podcast" actually existed (as it predated the release of the Apple iPod). It ran from 1998 to 2004 and consisted of 196 episodes. It covered various topics relating to the games made by Decipher, from design tidbits to interviews to spoilers to DecipherCon coverage.

Originally hosted by Carol Wisely (VP of Marketing), after episode 30 the regular guests Kyle Heuer and Evan Lorentz took over hosting duties.

As Decipher's own RFD page attested: 

Radio Free Decipher, or RFD, is the authentic voice of Decipher's Young Jedi, Austin Powers, Star Trek and Star Wars Customizable Card Games and the people who make them. We've been broadcasting since December, 1998, with special broadcasts from the road and from DecipherCon.
RFD connects you to information that you won't get anywhere else. Longtime listeners often hear new product news before it's mentioned anywhere else. That's because RFD is casual and fun, as well as authoritative. Listen up. You'll be glad you did.


Decipher WARS Radio[edit]

(YouTube playlist here.)

Following the final episode of RFD on 30 April 2004 (which coincidentally announced and discussed the WARS TCG with Decipher VP Tim Ellington), the resources for RFD were re-routed into a more focused podcast which focused on showcasing the WARS TCG (rather than all active Decipher properties). This series ran for 24 episodes from 25 June 2004 to 21 January 2005, and was primarily hosted by Mark Tuttle (PR and Marketing Manager).

The recording and production quality were noticibly better (partially due to the move to MP3 instead of RealAudio).

The original archive can be found here: http://web.archive.org/web/20050207162310/http://warstcg.fanhq.com/Articles/Article.aspx?ID=329

Decipher Games Radio[edit]

(YouTube playlist here.)

With the end of DWR, one final attempt to create a podcast series began in March 2005, although it only lasted 4 episodes, 2 of which are lost to time. Mark Tuttle continued hosting, following a more general format that mimicked the earlier RFD topic spread.

Technical Issues[edit]

Many audio files from this era are truncated due to a technical issue that has existed since almost the entire lifetime of the podcast, as posted on their main info page for RFD: 

TECH NOTE:
Some listeners have problems with streaming Real Audio. The most common problem is that the stream stops abruptly, though the "radio controls" on your computer desktop indicate the file continues to play.
Until we can determine its causes, our solution is to give you alternate access to RFD that lets you bypass the problem by downloading audio files directly to your system. Choose the link below and handle these files as you do others that you download. File sizes range from 1.75-3.0 MB.

This truncation means that many files taken from the Wayback are cut off at exactly 1MB, or 8:43 of runtime.
